## Summary
Abstract Fast and accurate chemical shielding estimators are essential for shielding-driven Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) crystallography. Machine-learning models for shielding predictions have matured significantly and today are primarily limited by the electronic structure reference data they are trained on. Here, we introduce ShiftML4, a shielding-tensor model trained directly on monomer-corrected calculations that approximate PBE0, rather than the PBE reference targeted by earlier ShiftML models. ShiftML4 is trained on a diverse set of structures containing 12 of the most common NMR nuclei in molecular organic solids. On experimental benchmarks and identical geometries, ShiftML4 reduces the 13C isotropic shift prediction error by about 30% with respect to both ShiftML3 and GIPAW-PBE, and the 15N error by about 15% with respect to ShiftML3, while the 1H accuracy is essentially unchanged. Prediction errors of chemical shielding anisotropies also improve by 20% over GIPAW-PBE on 13C principal components. Combined with PET-MOLS, a transferable machine-learned interatomic potential for organic crystals that can generate approximate hybrid-DFT geometries of small crystals in seconds, ShiftML4 lowers the 13C and 15N prediction errors by about 40% and 50% relative to ShiftML3 on GGA geometries, yielding final prediction accuracies of 1.5 and 3.7 ppm, respectively.
