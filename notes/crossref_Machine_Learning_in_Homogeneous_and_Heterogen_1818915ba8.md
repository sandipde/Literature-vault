## Summary
Abstract Machine learning (ML) is now applied across every stage of catalysis research, yet its track record differs sharply between homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysis. This perspective examines the structural reasons behind this divergence. We argue that homogeneous catalysis offers ML a more favorable setting for experimental optimization: discrete molecular catalysts, well-defined descriptors, precise measurables such as enantioselectivity and yield, and mature high-throughput experimentation (HTE) platforms. Heterogeneous catalysis, by contrast, has proven more successful for computational screening at scale, drawing on massive DFT datasets (>106 relaxations) and established physics-informed frameworks such as adsorption-energy scaling relations. The contrast is one of complementary strengths, not overall quality. The three classical gaps of heterogeneous catalysis (pressure, materials, and complexity) remain the main barriers preventing ML from closing the feedback loop between computation and experiment. We discuss how self-driving laboratories, operando characterization, and foundation models may drive convergence and identify what each field can learn from the other.
