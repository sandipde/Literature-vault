## Summary
Although deep learning has achieved remarkable success in various fields, most of these advances rely on large-scale well-annotated datasets. In real-world applications, however, collecting labeled data is often expensive and time-consuming. Active Learning (AL) has emerged as a promising solution that mitigates labeling costs by selectively querying the most informative instances for annotation. In particular, hybrid AL methods that integrate multiple acquisition criteria such as uncertainty, diversity, or representativeness into a joint function have been gaining attention. In this paper, we propose a novel hybrid active learning method named Langevin Density Estimation for Active Data Selection, or LEAD, that explicitly models representativeness through density estimation. Rather than relying on heuristic similarity or indirect distribution matching, LEAD estimates the unlabeled feature distribution through Langevin-based density estimation and assigns high representativeness scores to instances in high-density regions. Simultaneously, a diversity score, measured as the distance to the nearest labeled instance, ensures coverage of low-density regions. Quantitative and qualitative analyses demonstrate the effectiveness of LEAD in selecting diverse and representative samples under a limited labeling budget, compared to the baselines.
