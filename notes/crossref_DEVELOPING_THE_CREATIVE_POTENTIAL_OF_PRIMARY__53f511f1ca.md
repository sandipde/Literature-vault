## Summary
This article examines the development of primary school pupils’ creative potential through an integrative analysis of major theories of creativity, recent systematic reviews in primary education, and the OECD PISA 2022 creative thinking framework. Eighteen scholarly and official sources published between 1950 and 2024 were purposively selected using explicit relevance and quality criteria. The analysis integrates the novelty–appropriateness conception of creativity, indicators of divergent thinking, classroom-context factors, task characteristics, and information-processing processes. On this basis, an integrative methodological sequence is proposed for primary education: understanding information; selecting and reorganising it; identifying a problem; generating alternatives; selecting an original and appropriate idea; justifying it; and evaluating and improving it. A grade-sensitive matrix for Grades 1–4 is also proposed to support progression from simple idea generation to perspective shifting, justification, and refinement. PISA 2022 findings are treated as a system-level signal rather than as direct evidence about primary pupils. The framework remains conceptual and requires classroom validation, but it provides a theoretically grounded basis for designing creative tasks and formative assessment in primary education.
