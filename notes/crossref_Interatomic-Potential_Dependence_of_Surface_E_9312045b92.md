# Interatomic-Potential Dependence of Surface Elasticity in Silicon Nanobeams: A Molecular Dynamics Calibration under Bending and Free Vibration

## Summary
Surface elasticity plays an important role in the size-dependent mechanical behavior of silicon nanostructures, yet its atomistic-to-continuum characterization may depend on the interatomic potential used in molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. This study systematically investigates the influence of five silicon interatomic potentials-Stillinger-Weber, original Tersoff, modified Tersoff, Lenosky MEAM, and EDIP-on the surface-elastic response of silicon nanobeams under static bending and free transverse vibration. The Miller-Shenoy surface elasticity framework is employed to independently calibrate the characteristic surface-elastic length scale (ℎ 𝑜) for each potential and mechanical response. Potential-specific bulk elastic properties are determined separately to distinguish variations in bulk stiffness from nanoscale surface effects. The calculated [100] Young's modulus ranges from 50.74 to 136.60 GPa, compared with the experimental value of 130.0 GPa, demonstrating substantial sensitivity to the atomistic description. The calibrated surface-elastic length scale also varies markedly among potentials, while the accuracy of the continuum model depends on both the potential and loading mode. Stillinger-Weber provides the most accurate overall calibration, whereas EDIP exhibits pronounced loading-mode sensitivity, with bending and vibration mean absolute percentage errors of 10.65% and 2.44%, respectively.
