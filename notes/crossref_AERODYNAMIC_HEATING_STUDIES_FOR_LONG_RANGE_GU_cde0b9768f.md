## Summary
Aerodynamic heating is the one of the challenging aspect in the design of supersonic/hypersonic rockets. The long-range missiles will be airborne for longer durations, which exposes the airframe for the higher temperatures. Therefore, it becomes extremely important to estimate the airframe skin temperatures. The guidance, navigation and control (GNC) electronic components need to sustain the high temperatures during complete trajectory. The airframe material strength will also depend on the skin temperatures. The structural factor of safety will be lesser if the temperatures cross threshold limits. The present study is based on Jerger Equations to estimate heat transfer coefficient and skin temperature with the consideration of material properties. Most of the missiles have relatively short time of flight duration; therefore, the heat transfer is usually in a transient mode rather than steady state condition. Aerodynamic heating predication is typically carried out with the two approaches thermal thin and thermally thick surface. A thermally thin surface is approximated as one dimensional heat transfer with nearly uniform internal temperature that increases with time and eventually reaches to recovery temperature. While, thermally thick surface is approximated as one-dimensional heat transfer through an infinite slab. These both approaches are used in present study for estimation of skin temperatures at various sections over the complete rocket body.
