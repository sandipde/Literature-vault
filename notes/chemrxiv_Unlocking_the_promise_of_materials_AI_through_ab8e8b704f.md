## Summary
AI-guided and robotic laboratories can accelerate experiments within a company, but materials development still depends on repeated testing across company boundaries. Here we show in controlled simulation how investment in application-relevant private testing reduces that external work. We extend Minimum Knowledge Collaboration with application proxies, calling the extension MKC2. Suppliers develop combinations of physical tests and reference assemblies that capture how their materials respond under relevant product conditions. They train private models on the resulting curves and features, and the product integrator calibrates joint predictions against complete-product tests. Bidirectional MKC messages connect private material selection to the product requirements while raw curves and model parameters remain local. In sixteen paired two-company programs, 72 additional private training curves per program accompany a 93% reduction in subsequent product testing at a target of 90% of the best available combination. Target attainment rises from twelve to all sixteen programs within the fixed test horizon. Including the shared initial product campaign gives a 39% reduction. Matched two- and four-company studies and a reference-training sensitivity study examine the same exchange under prescribed physical response families. The benefit depends on developing informative tests, training useful predictions and acting on them in collaboration. AI and robotics can make the private investment faster. MKC2 connects that application learning to fewer tests across company boundaries.
