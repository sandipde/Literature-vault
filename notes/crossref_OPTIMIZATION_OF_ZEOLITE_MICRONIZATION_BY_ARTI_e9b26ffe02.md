## Summary
Natural clinoptilolite zeolite was subjected to micronization in a laboratory vibrating ring mill to determine optimal grinding conditions and evaluate the applicability of artificial neural networks (ANNs) for process prediction. Four particle size classes (-3.35+0 mm, -3.35+2.36 mm, -2.36+1.18 mm and -1.18+0 mm) and three feed masses (50, 100 and 200 g) were investigated at grinding times ranging from 20 to 900 s. The grinding products were characterized through the content of the fine size fraction (-5+0 μm) and specific surface area. A Multi-Layer Perceptron neural network was developed using particle size class, grinding time and feed mass as input variables. The obtained results showed that grinding times between 45 and 75 s provided the highest degree of micronization and maximum specific surface area values. Longer grinding periods resulted in particle agglomeration and a decrease in grinding efficiency. The ANN model successfully predicted the process responses with correlation coefficients ranging from 0.989 to 0.997 and prediction errors below 6%. The results demonstrate that ANN modeling can be successfully applied for optimization and prediction of zeolite micronization processes.
