## Summary
Key Points A systematic audit of six leading journals identifies 301 documented submission requirements, none of which directly evaluates the scientific validity of a manuscript. Thirty‐six of these requirements carry conflicting mandatory values across journals, so full compliance with one journal can create measurable non‐compliance with another. This non‐scientific compliance burden is estimated to consume 24 million hours of global scientific labour annually, falling disproportionately on independent and unaffiliated researchers. Three targeted interventions—a submission transparency index, portable peer review and format‐neutral initial submission—could reduce this burden without altering editorial standards or peer review.
