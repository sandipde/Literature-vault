## Summary
Abstract Selective hydrogenation of acetylene to ethylene at temperatures relevant to direct non-oxidative methane conversion (DNMC) could enable downstream upgrading without intermediate cooling and reheating. Here, we combine experimental catalyst screening, machine learning potential (MLP) simulations, and density functional theory (DFT) calculations to identify Pd surface motifs responsible for acetylene hydrogenation at 850°C. Among cristobalite-supported catalysts, Pd exhibited the highest hydrocarbon formation rate (~ 3.0 × 10 3 µmol-C g cat ⁻¹ h⁻¹), high ethylene productivity, low coke formation, and negligible deactivation. MLP screening of 31 Pd surfaces revealed that Pd(100)-like 4-fold hollow sites bind acetylene substantially more strongly than Pd(111)-like 3-fold sites. DFT calculations with high temperature entropy corrections confirmed favorable acetylene adsorption on Pd(100) and several high-index surfaces exposing 4-fold motifs at 1123 K, whereas adsorption on Pd(111) was unfavorable. Tensile strain up to 2.0% had only a minor effect. On Pd(100)-2.0%, sequential hydrogenation to C 2 H 3 * and C 2 H 4 * required activation free energies of 1.03 and 1.02 eV, while competing C–C coupling and dehydrogenation pathways had higher barriers. These results identify Pd(100)-like 4-fold ensembles as key motifs for high temperature acetylene-to-ethylene conversion and demonstrate the utility of integrating experimental screening with MLP-assisted surface analysis and DFT.
