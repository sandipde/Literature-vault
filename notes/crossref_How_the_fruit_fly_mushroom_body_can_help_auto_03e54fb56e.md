# How the fruit fly mushroom body can help autonomous materials discovery

## Summary
Self-driving laboratories plan well but react slowly: a failing run, finished conversion or odd reading needs a call within seconds, at the instrument. We built that reflex stage by stage from the fruit-fly odor circuit. Raman bands become odor channels; a fixed number of cells fire, keeping shape, not strength. An output compartment learns to stop after one failed and one good run, and keeps learning. In simulated selenization and argon-ion irradiation of 2D semiconductors (20 repeats), one fixed-setting layer made four calls. It needs no retraining over a campaign and its memory does not grow, yet a rare phase from one burst was recognized on an early return. The stop cut flakes lost from 7.75 to 3.55 of 30, level with tuned tools. In a simulator from four published spectra, three flakes gave it slightly lower dose error than an equal-memory raw-spectrum lookup. Ending furnace runs at the instrument lost 0.10 flakes, against 11.0 waiting an assumed 60 s for a planner. Engineered specialists, one per call, are smaller, flag odd readings better and run 24-fold faster. Laboratories can split decisions in two: the planner keeps the recipe; a small fly-circuit layer at each instrument makes the fast calls.
