## Summary
We compare scalar and electronic-overlap reductions of a published three-state, two-coordinate benzene-cation model with fixed Jahn–Teller and pseudo-Jahn–Teller couplings. An analytic oscillator-tail bound and a residual-controlled finite-matrix certificate prove that its lowest orbital vibronic eigenvalue is exactly twofold and transforms as 𝐸 . By ground-state uniqueness, no adjustment of a finite real confining scalar potential with the ordinary single-valued nuclear kinetic operator can recover this multiplicity. An electronic-overlap projection restores the low-energy doublet pattern and yields a first distinct excitation near 103.3 cm−1, versus 102.4989 cm−1 for the full three-state model; the scalar reduction instead has a nondegenerate ground state and a 76.61 cm−1 first excitation. Independently, we classify every finite conical intersection and its participating electronic sheets, derive all three electronic eigenline phase signs from loop winding, and determine the exact parameter threshold separating minima inside and outside the satellite intersections. Preparation-resolved response calculations show that symmetry-allowed gaps are not automatically excited: the 102.50 and 159.38 cm−1 ground-doublet transitions are absent for a specified incoherent Franck–Condon mixture, whereas an approximately 58.94 cm−1 coherence contributes to a radial probe. These results define a verifiable boundary for scalar-potential reductions and provide state-preparation criteria for future spectroscopic tests, without assigning the reported experimental oscillation to the reduced model.
