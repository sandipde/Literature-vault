# Site-Dependent Promotion of CO Hydrogenation on Rh(211): Role of Impurity Stability and Step-Edge Charge Distribution

## Summary
First-principles calculations were used to investigate direct CO hydrogenation along the step-edge of the Rh(211) surface, modified by substitutional 3d transition metal impurities (X = Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, and Cu) occupying either the step-edge or the terrace lattice site. Impurities at the step-edge site substantially lower the activation barrier relative to pristine Rh(211), with Ti inducing the lowest energy barrier of 0.80 eV, followed by Cr, V, and Mn. The enhanced promotion associated with the step-edge impurities correlates with a dipole-like charge distribution between neighboring step-edge atoms. Results revealed that while Ti, V, and Cr are highly active at the step-edge site, these metals segregate to the terrace site, whereas Mn and Fe are nearly thermoneutral between step-edge and terrace sites and can still provide a reduction of CO hydrogenation barriers. MnMn and FeFe models, with same element impurities co-substituted at both step-edge and terrace lattice sites, further reduce the energy barriers to 0.95 and 1.00 eV, respectively, relative to their corresponding single-impurity models. These demonstrate that effective promotion of CO hydrogenation on Rh(211) requires direct step-edge site participation, thermodynamic accessibility of the active impurity configuration, and local charge polarization at the active sites.
