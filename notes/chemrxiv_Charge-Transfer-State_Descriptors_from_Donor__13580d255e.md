## Summary
Organic photovoltaic (OPV) devices have attracted significant attention as lightweight, flexible, and solution-processable alternatives to conventional solar cells. In this work, a novel computational workflow was developed to generate donor–acceptor interfacial adducts for non-fullerene acceptor (NFA)-based indoor OPV systems and to investigate their charge-transfer (CT) characteristics using density functional theory (DFT) and time-dependent DFT calculations. Molecular docking followed by excited-state analysis enabled identification of important CT descriptors, including excitation energies, electron–hole separation, participation ratios, and charge-transfer characteristics. A dataset containing 30 donor–acceptor pairs were subsequently constructed by combining these CT descriptors with molecular fingerprints and frontier molecular orbital (FMO) parameters. Machine learning (ML) models based on ElasticNet and XGBoost algorithms were then trained to predict OPV power conversion efficiency (PCE). Our results demonstrate that incorporating physically meaningful CT descriptors derived from donor–acceptor interfaces significantly improve the ML-based prediction of OPV efficiencies from a small dataset.
