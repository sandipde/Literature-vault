## Summary
Clay minerals are widely assumed to retard per-and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in soils, yet the strength and mechanism of PFAS binding to smectites are unresolved: measured montmorillonite Kd values span two orders of magnitude, and the only published free-energy simulation of PFAS on smectite predicts logKd values higher than any measurement. We combined umbrella-sampling molecular dynamics of ten legacy and emerging PFAS (C4–C8 perfluoroalkyl carboxylates and sulfonates, GenX, ADONA, 6:2 FTS and F-53B) on Na-and Ca-montmorillonite with a Gaussian process regression (GPR) model of soil Kd and a vadose-zone leaching screen. On an evenly charged basal surface, binding is weak for every compound (free-energy minima between +0.6 and −9.8 kJmol−1), stronger with Ca2+ than with Na+ for nine of ten PFAS, and net exclusion from the surface occurs for six of ten PFAS on Namontmorillonite. Two independent force fields agree within 0.6 kJmol−1 for PFBS on Ca-montmorillonite, and a stiff-restraint contact test finds no deeper inner-sphere state. Concentrating the layer charge into patches deepens binding by 4 kJmol−1 for PFBS and 12 kJmol−1 for PFOS, with the molecule residing on the uncharged siloxane patch;
