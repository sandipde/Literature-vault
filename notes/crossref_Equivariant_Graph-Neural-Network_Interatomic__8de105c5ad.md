## Summary
Additively manufactured (AM) refractory high-entropy alloys are candidate fusion plasma-facing materials that must withstand cascades and implanted hydrogen and helium. To enable an atomistic test of whether AM microstructure and high-entropy chemistry together strengthen the radiation tolerance of W₃₈Ta₃₆Cr₁₅V₁₁, proposed for this route, we develop from scratch and compare four graph-neural-network (GNN) potentials for W–Ta–Cr–V–H–He: two equivariant MACE models, a lightweight production model and a full-capacity reference, and the invariant DPA-2 and DPA-3. Each is trained with its own recipe on a purpose-built density-functional-theory (DFT) database of surfaces, pores and gases, published metallic data and, except for DPA-2, analytic short-range references, with screened-Coulomb repulsion. To our knowledge, the production model is the first MACE potential applied to cascades in a high-entropy alloy, and the four are the first GNN potentials validated for radiation damage with hydrogen and helium chemistry in an alloy proposed for AM. The production model reproduces the tungsten elastic tensor within 4.3% of DFT, passes all 21 short-range element-pair tests with its deployment switch and none without it, remains stable in collision cascades, and binds helium more strongly than hydrogen at nanovoid walls, more deeply in larger cavities, as in DFT. It nearly matches the full-capacity model's energy accuracy with a fivefold larger single-GPU workload. Both equivariant models pass every pair test and reproduce the cavity-size trend, whereas the invariant models pass only 14 and 9 and overstate it. The potentials enable large-scale simulation of radiation damage and helium retention in this alloy under prototypical fusion conditions.
