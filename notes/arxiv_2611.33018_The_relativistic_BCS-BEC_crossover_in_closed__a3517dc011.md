# The relativistic BCS-BEC crossover in closed form: the cutoff as an effective range

- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33018

## Summary
At zero temperature the mean-field potential of a Dirac fermion with a pairing gap contains one momentum integral, that of the sum of its two quasiparticle levels. We evaluate it in closed form from a geometric construction: the two levels are distances in a plane, and the momentum line is a finite arc of a fixed ellipse, along which the charge, the scalar density and the pair amplitude, summed over the modes, are combinations of Legendre's three elliptic integrals. As the upper limit of the momentum grows, the integral grows by a quartic term, a quadratic term and a logarithm with a known coefficient. The two powers are absorbed into a constant and into the coupling, and with all momenta integrated what remains is a finite combination of Legendre's integrals and the logarithm of one scale $λ$. The gap equation and the density of the relativistic BCS-BEC crossover follow in closed form. Once the coupling has been traded for the scattering length, $λ$ takes the place of the cutoff that the literature keeps as a third parameter. We show that it fixes the effective range of the channel of the pair: for a pair of Dirac fermions of mass $m$ with the pairing vertex $γ_5$, $r_e=-(4/πm)[\ln(2λ/m)-1]$. At low density and a fixed $k_Fr_e$ the crossover is that of the two-channel model of a Feshbach resonance with the same scattering length and effective range. In the Nambu-Jona-Lasinio model the pion decay constant fixes $λ$, the effective range of the pion channel is $-8πf_π^{2}/(N_cM^{3})$, and the constituent mass $M$ of the quark is the one parameter that remains free.
