# Electrochemistry in air enabled by electric-field spillover at three-phase interfaces

- **Published:** 2026-11-16

## Summary
Electric-field spillover at dielectric/electrolyte/electrode interfaces drives electrochemical reactions in traditionally forbidden environments, including insulating liquids and gases. Here, we show that ferrocene present in either air or toluene undergoes concerted electron- and phase-transfer at the molecularly thin 3-phase boundary, resulting in its oxidation and the transfer of the reaction product, ferrocenium, into the aqueous phase. A detailed investigation of the thermodynamic and kinetic properties of these reactions reveals that concerted electron- and phase-transfer in air proceeds substantially faster than in the liquid dielectric, toluene. These remarkable differences are the result of smaller solvation constraints, shorter electron-tunneling distance, stronger electronic coupling, and faster diffusion of reactants. Our findings establish electrochemistry in air as a platform for rapid interfacial reactions.
