# Multi-output neural network surrogate for double- pulse femtosecond optothermal control of thin silicon films on fused silica

## Summary
<p dir="ltr">We introduce a multi-output feedforward neural network surrogate trained on a coupled multiple-reflection and two-temperature model to map double-pulse femtosecond laser excitation (1030 nm) of crystalline silicon films on fused silica across film thicknesses (50-500 nm) and inter-pulse delays (0-10ps). Trained via Levenberg-Marquardt optimization on 2000 physical simulations, the dual-hidden-layer network ([40,30] neurons) simultaneously predicts six strongly coupled optothermal observables, peak electron temperature, peak lattice temperature, peak carrier density, minimum and maximum absorptivity and minimum refractive index achieving coefficients of determination R<sup>2</sup>≥0.9998 across all targets. Evaluating a dense 200×200 design grid in milliseconds resolves pronounced thin-film interference fringes and carrier-induced index modulation, identifying an optimal optothermal coupling regime at d=156 nm and Δτ=4.572 ps. This unified machine-learning workflow eliminates computationally expensive multi-physics runtime bottlenecks for real-time laser processing control and inverse photonic thin-film design.</p>
