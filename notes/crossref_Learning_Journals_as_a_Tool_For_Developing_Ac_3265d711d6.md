## Summary
The study was conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of using learning journals in developing active learning among Early Childhood Education students in the context of competency-oriented teacher training reform. The study employed a mixed-methods approach with 45 students enrolled in the course Learning and Psychological Development of Preschool-Age Children at Kien Giang College of Education. Data were collected through pre- and post-intervention surveys, pedagogical observations, analysis of learning journals, formative assessment, and student feedback. The study aimed to examine changes in several indicators of active learning among Early Childhood Education students after 12 weeks of using learning journals, while also exploring changes in self-assessment and self-regulation, as well as the advantages and difficulties experienced by students in maintaining the journaling activity. The results show that several indicators of students' active learning increased following the implementation of learning journals. The proportion of students who regularly prepared for lessons increased from 46.7% to 66.7%; 91.1% of students reported feeling interested or very interested in the course; 97.8% actively participated in speaking and discussions; and 100% rated learning journals as effective or highly effective in supporting self-assessment and adjustment of their learning. The findings indicate that learning journals can support several indicators of active learning while also creating opportunities for students to engage in self-assessment and self-regulation activities. The study proposes expanding the use of learning journals in teacher training courses, integrating them with formative assessment and regular instructor feedback to improve training quality, while continuing to validate the model on a larger scale and across other training disciplines.
