## Summary
Cycloparaphenylenes (CPPs) are among the most versatile carbon nanohoops for fullerene recognition. However, their structural, electronic, and host-guest properties are fixed by molecular composition. Here, we introduce a different concept by incorporation of a diarylethene (DAE) photoswitch into the CPP framework, thereby transforming a static carbon nanohoop into a light-responsive supramolecular host. DFT calculations reveal that photoisomerization of the incorporated DAE unit reversibly expands the nanohoop cavity by approximately 1 Å, enabling light-controlled switching of fullerene selectivity among C 60 , C 70 , and C 84 . Beyond structural changes, photoisomerization also reorganizes the electronic structure and excited-state properties of the host-guest complexes. Ring closure introduces a previously inaccessible DAE-centered charge-transfer (CT) state in addition to the conventional CPP-to-fullerene CT pathway. Although this new CT state is strongly stabilized in polar media, electron-transfer kinetics indicate that rapid charge recombination limits its population, whereas the conventional CT state remains long-lived. These findings suggest that incorporation of a DAE photoswitch enables reversible control over cavity size, guest selectivity, exciton localization, and electron-transfer dynamics, yielding adaptive, stimuli-responsive carbon nanohoops with broad potential in photoactive materials.
