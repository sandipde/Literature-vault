# Correlated chemical and displacive disorder in a lithium-rich disordered rocksalt revealed by electron diffuse scattering

- **Type:** arxiv
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30537
- **Captured by:** @auto-scanner
- **Metadata:** complete (arXiv)
- **Authors:** Sam Y. Thompson, Kristoffer A. H. Støckler, Nikolaos Zacharakis, Rebekka Klemmt, Ella M. Schmidt, Sandro Wieser, Nico Unglert, Jørgen Skibsted, Espen D. Bøjesen, Georg K. H. Madsen, Bo B. Iversen, Dorthe B. Ravnsbæk
- **Published:** 2026-11-23

## Summary
Lithium transport in disordered rocksalt cathodes depends on local cation arrangements and atomic distortions that are hidden from average-structure analysis. Resolving both contributions experimentally remains challenging, particularly in powders where directional information is lost. Here, we use electron diffuse scattering and three-dimensional difference pair distribution function analysis (3D-ΔPDF) to resolve occupational and displacive correlations in the model lithium-rich rocksalt Li{_2.5}TiO{_3}F{_0.5}. We identify an unusual combination of like-cation first-neighbour and unlike-cation second-neighbour preferences with correlated anion displacements towards Ti and accompanying cation displacements. A minimal pair-interaction model incorporating these displacements reproduces the principal diffuse-scattering and 3D-ΔPDF features and improves agreement with powder-PDF measurements. The experimentally constrained cation configurations exhibit an above-random population of 0-TM lithium-migration environments. These results establish electron 3D-ΔPDF as a route to constrain atomistic models incorporating chemical order and local relaxation, providing a foundation for assessing their combined influence on lithium migration in disordered rocksalt materials.
