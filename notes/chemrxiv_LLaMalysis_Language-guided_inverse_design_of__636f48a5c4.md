## Summary
Computational catalyst discovery is constrained by vast compositional and structural spaces, whereas atomistic generative models typically rely on engineered conditioning variables. Here we formulate catalyst inverse design as an instruction-following task in which compositional and catalytic requirements are expressed in natural language. We fine-tune an instruction-tuned language model on serialized catalyst slabs from the Open Catalyst 2020 dataset and obtain a generator with validity and uniqueness comparable to catalyst-specific baselines. Reward-weighted regression using machine-learning interatomic-potential feedback increases the number of candidates satisfying the validity criteria and lying within 0.10 eV of the target from 8 to a maximum of 65 per 500 generations. The refined model retains metal-specific controllability for ten metals, and density functional theory calculations of 101 prioritized candidates identify multiple alloy families with promising hydrogen adsorption energies. These results establish natural language as a practical conditioning interface for atomistic catalyst design.
