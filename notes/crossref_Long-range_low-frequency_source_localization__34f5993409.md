## Summary
In late April of 2023, a pair of acoustic transceivers (SIO N and SIO E) were deployed North of the Atlantis II seamount in the North Atlantic Ocean roughly 153-km apart. They exchanged hourly, 250 Hz, broadband transmissions for 24 hours before SIO E failed due to a catastrophic Gulf Stream mooring blowdown. Twenty-three recordings of the SIO E source by the SIO N receiver were obtained on two large-aperture vertical subarrays, nominally spanning 1100-m to 850-m and 340-m to 90-m depth. The target transceiver source depths were 1100-m but because of the SIO E failure its precise position due to mooring motion is not known. Furthermore, the precise location of the SIO N receiver due to motion is not known though pressure sensors monitored its depth. The observed timefront arrival patterns reveal 4 ducted arrivals and up to 20 bottom/surface interacting paths. Using ray theory along with iterative Bayesian methods and CTDs from the mooring deployments and a surface to 1100-m depth CTD section carried out along the path in late May, we utilize boundary interacting arrival time geometric dispersion to localize SIO E in depth and range to with RMS uncertainties of 16 m and 223 m respectively for both the upper and lower arrays. Treating the receiver depth as an unknown, we can verify its depth using the pressure sensors. Sensitivities of the localization to number of arrivals utilized, environmental mismatch, and initial guess are investigated.
