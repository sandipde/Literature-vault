# Ultrafast Charge Separation Controlled by Aromatic Guests in a Perylene Dimer

## Summary
Engineering charge separation and recombination through non-covalent interactions offers a powerful route to functional photoactive materials without the need for covalent re-engineering. Here, we investigate the ultrafast excited-state dynamics of host-guest complexes formed between a valinol-bridged perylene diimide (PDI) dimer macrocycle and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) guests using sub-50 femtosecond time resolution transient absorption spectroscopy. Macrocycle complexation with pyrene, perylene, and coronene introduces efficient charge-transfer pathways absent in the free host. Photoexcitation of the PDI dimer host leads to rapid quenching of stimulated emission and sub-picosecond formation of PDI radical anions, providing direct evidence for ultrafast hole transfer from the excited host to the PAH guest. The resulting charge-separated states persist for hundreds of picoseconds to nanoseconds before recombination. Analysis within the Marcus framework reveals that charge separation proceeds in a near-barrierless regime, whereas charge recombination lies deep in the Marcus inverted region, accounting for long-lived radical pair states. These results establish aromatic guest binding as a non-covalent handle for accessing fast hole transfer enabling long-lived charge-separated states in supramolecular donor-acceptor assemblies, relevant to light harvesting, photoredox catalysis and organic electronics.
