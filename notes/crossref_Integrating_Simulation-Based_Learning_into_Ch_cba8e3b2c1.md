## Summary
This study examines how simulation-based learning (SBL) can be integrated into challenge-based learning (CBL) through the experiential learning cycle in industrial engineering education. Although SBL and CBL are recognised as effective pedagogies, guidance on systematically embedding simulations into challenge-oriented environments remains limited. To address this, this study proposes and applies a framework that integrates SBL, CBL, and experiential learning using the Pro-Game simulator for production sequencing and combinatorial optimisation problems. A mixed-methods exploratory case study was conducted within an undergraduate industrial engineering module with 38 students. The framework was implemented through challenge-based simulation activities structured around concrete experience, reflective observation, abstract conceptualisation, and active experimentation. Descriptive findings indicated favourable self-reported motivation towards the simulation activities and high instructor-assessed competency attainment, while the institutional survey reflected positive student evaluations of the module as a whole. This study contributes theoretically by proposing a coherent integration of SBL, CBL, and experiential learning and practically by guiding the embedding of simulations into challenge-based engineering education. However, this study is limited by its single-case design, reliance on student descriptive and self-reported data, and the absence of longitudinal or comparative analyses. Future research should evaluate this framework across disciplines and contexts and examine long-term learning outcomes and broader competencies.
