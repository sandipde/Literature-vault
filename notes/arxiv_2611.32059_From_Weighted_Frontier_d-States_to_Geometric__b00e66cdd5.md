# From Weighted Frontier d-States to Geometric Descriptors for Atomically Dispersed Catalysts at the Molecule-Solid Crossover

- **Type:** arxiv
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32059
- **Captured by:** @auto-scanner
- **Metadata:** complete (arXiv)
- **Authors:** Yuqi Li, Zewen Wu, Longling Pang, Zhidan Li, Xianghua Kong
- **Published:** 2026-11-24

## Summary
Atomically dispersed catalysts exhibit molecule-solid crossover character when localized metal orbitals hybridize with support-derived bands, a regime not fully captured by either a d-band-center description or a frontier-orbital picture. We establish an electronic-to-geometric framework for oxygen reduction across fourteen metals. N-to-P substitution in M-N4 sites raises theoretical onset potentials in 13 systems, with Rh-N3P reaching 0.88 V, comparable to the Pt(111) benchmark. Scaling relations reduce *OOH, *O, and *OH thermodynamics to $Δ$G*OH. M-N4 follows a dyz-dominated frontier-orbital picture, whereas M-N3P requires a weighted frontier d-state representation built from adsorption-relevant orbitals and their occupancies. The resulting $ε$WFDS correlates with $Δ$G*OH at $R^2$ = 0.92, compared with 0.83 for the d-band center and 0.64 for the frontier-orbital. The identified roles of orbital occupancy and local coordination suggest an interpretable geometric descriptor, $ζ$M-N3P = nd/rM-P, that predicts $Δ$G*OH at $R^2$ = 0.96. The ORR volcano analysis identifies strong-binding systems and motivates axial oxygen coordination. This design raises onset potentials by 0.70-2.46 V, and the M-N3PO descriptor predicts $Δ$G*OH at $R^2$ = 0.91. By bridging frontier-orbital and d-band-center descriptions, the framework provides a physically grounded electronic representation and a geometry-based screening route compatible with machine-learned interatomic potentials.
