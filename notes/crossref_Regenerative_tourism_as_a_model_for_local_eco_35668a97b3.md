## Summary
The purpose of this study is to explore the potential of regenerative tourism as a model for restoring local ecosystems, with particular emphasis on the possibilities for its application in Serbia. Unlike traditional and sustainable tourism, regenerative tourism focuses not only on reducing negative environmental impacts but also on actively restoring natural resources. The study identifies the main mechanisms through which tourism can contribute to ecological restoration while also supporting the local economy and community. A qualitative analysis of relevant scientific literature was used to examine the ecological, economic, and social effects of regenerative tourism, as well as the key financial, institutional, and organizational constraints that may affect its implementation. Particular attention was given to the role of circular economy principles, local community participation, and coordination among key stakeholders in achieving the regenerative effects of tourism. Based on the analysis, a conceptual model for regenerative tourism in Serbia was proposed, linking tourism activities with restoration measures and outcome indicators. The research results showed that the potential of regenerative tourism depends on local ecological and socio-economic conditions, long-term planning, and clearly defined and measurable restoration objectives.
