# Machine-learned potentials for iron oxides at extreme conditions

- **Type:** arxiv
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24525
- **Captured by:** @auto-scanner
- **Metadata:** complete (arXiv)
- **Authors:** S. Azadi, S. M. Vinko, T. D. Kühne, A. Principi, M. S. Bahramy
- **Published:** 2026-11-18

## Summary
Reliable phase stability requires accurate relative free energies, which small force errors alone do not guarantee. We develop atomic-cluster-expansion potentials for five iron oxides from Fe$_2$O to Fe$_2$O$_3$, covering crystalline and liquid states at 140--260\,GPa and 1000--11000\,K, with electronic and ionic temperatures matched. Force-dominated training leaves systematic energy offsets between structure families of up to 41\,meV/atom that persist despite additional training data. Stronger energy weighting and weaker regularization largely remove these offsets, while converged k-point sampling prevents further composition-dependent biases. The final potentials achieve held-out energy errors of 2.0--5.5\,meV/atom and first-order free-energy errors within 3.4\,meV/atom for liquids at 5000--9000\,K. However, an untrained NiAs-type FeO phase exhibits a free-energy error of 57\,meV/atom despite a low extrapolation grade. These results show that reliable phase-diagram calculations require control of relative energy offsets and explicit free-energy validation of every competing phase.
