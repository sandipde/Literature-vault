## Summary
In forensic entomology, accurately identifying empty fly puparia remains challenging because the few unambiguous morphological characters used for identification are often damaged in nature or during sampling and storage and are not appropriately preserved. This study utilizes untargeted chemical fingerprinting using attenuated total reflectance Fourier-transform infrared (ATR-FTIR) spectroscopy with machine learning to generate accurate species identification models for empty fly puparia. A total of 1620 specimens from four forensically relevant blow fly species, Lucilia sericata, Calliphora vicina, Chrysomya albiceps, and Protophormia terraenovae, were analyzed using a full factorial experimental design with varying storage temperatures, storage times, and cleaning procedures to account for differences in forensic sample handling and preservation. FTIR spectra were preprocessed using different workflows, which included asymmetric least squares (ALS) or rubberband baseline correction, Savitzky–Golay smoothing, and standard normal variate (SNV) or MinMax normalization. The preprocessed FTIR spectra were used to generate different machine learning models using linear discriminant analysis (LDA), linear support vector machine (SVM), partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA), and random forest (RF) utilizing both full and reduced spectral ranges. The generated machine learning models produced high classification accuracy ranging from 87.1–100%. The ALS-SNV baseline correction-normalization preprocessing workflow generated the best results, while LDA and linear SVM models had the highest classification performance across all preprocessing workflows and spectral ranges, with accuracies reaching 100%. The maintained classification performance after spectral reduction shows that most of the spectral regions contributing to species discrimination in the models were retained. Variable importance analysis of the linear SVM model revealed high contributions from spectral regions associated with C–H stretching, O–H/N–H stretching, and amide bonds, suggesting that cuticular hydrocarbons and the chitin–protein complex, both major chemical constituents of empty puparia, contribute to species identification. External validation using independently collected 3–4-month indoor-weathered puparia resulted in classification accuracies of 92.5–100% across the evaluated models. These findings support the growing potential of ATR-FTIR spectroscopy with machine learning for rapid, inexpensive, and accurate species identification of empty fly puparia and its application to forensic entomology.
