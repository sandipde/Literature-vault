## Summary
The compression–shear fracture of quasi-brittle materials involves crack closure, frictional sliding and residual shear resistance, whereas the original nonlocal macro–meso-scale consistent damage (NMMD) model cannot capture compression–shear and mixed tension/compression–shear failure. This paper proposes an improved NMMD model for compression–shear-dominated fracture. Potential shear failure planes are determined using the Mohr–Coulomb criterion. Material-point-pair relative displacements are projected onto these planes to construct a nonlocal shear strain measure and a compression stress-dependent critical shear damage threshold. A shear topological damage variable is defined via the history-dependent maximum overshoot, and a pressure-sensitive energy degradation function is formulated for the residual shear strength. The total strain energy density is decomposed into tensile, compressive–volumetric and compressive–deviatoric components. Separate energy degradation mechanisms for tension and compression–shear damage establish the mixed-mode fracture constitutive relation. Implemented within an explicit finite element framework, the model is validated by direct shear and uniaxial compression benchmark simulations of pre-flawed specimens. The numerical results show that the model reproduces shear slip surfaces, tensile wing cracks and the pressure-dependent post-peak residual bearing capacity, providing a feasible tool for the mixed-mode fracture analysis of rock- and concrete-like quasi-brittle materials.
