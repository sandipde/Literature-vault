# Understanding a Helix Reverse Path in Perfluoroalkanes from a Neural Network Potential

## Summary
The helical handedness of perfluoroalkanes and polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) is considered to reverse through localized helix-reversal defects. Earlier studies with empirical and semiempirical potentials described how such defects form and move. The helix, however, originates from hyperconjugation, and the reversal pathway of a single molecule has not yet been examined with a potential that includes this quantum-chemical effect. Here we trained a MACE potential on DF-MP2-F12 data for C4F10–C7F16 and located three reversal pathways of C24F50 by CI-NEB: a concerted pathway through the all-trans (D2h) structure, a single-wall pathway, and a twin-wall pathway. For C24F50, the concerted barrier is 13.74 kcalmol−1, far above those of the single-wall pathway (2.01 kcalmol−1) and the twin-wall pathway (4.82 kcalmol−1); the concerted barrier grows linearly with chain length, whereas the single-wall barrier does not depend on it. At 25 ◦C, the single-wall pathway is about 108 times faster than the concerted one for C24F50 when the prefactors are assumed equal, so the concerted reversal of an isolated chain is kinetically very unfavorable. For C13F28–C15F32, DFT calculations show that the frequency of the wall mode depends on the position of the wall, so wall-containing conformations can contribute to the broad defectrelated band in the ATR-IR spectra of these short-chain perfluoroalkanes. The growth of the low-wavenumber component from 20 to 70 ◦C is consistent with an increasing thermal population of these conformations. In the single-wall path, a moving wall also rotates the chain about its axis. Combined with crystal simulations in the literature, this supports the screw-like motion proposed for PTFE, in which the rotation of a chain is accompanied by a translation along its axis.
