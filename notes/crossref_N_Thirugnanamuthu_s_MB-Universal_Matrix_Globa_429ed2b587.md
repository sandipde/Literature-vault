## Summary
We present the MB-Universal Matrix, a matrix framework for modelling how global economic, technological, social and environmental indicators evolve and respond to external actors. The framework has three parts: a column-stochastic transition matrix A, a thirdorder impact tensor T that perturbs A, and a stochastic simulation layer. We prove that ρ(A) = 1, give a spectral convergence bound, and derive the ordinary and ridge least-squares estimators step by step. As a first empirical test, a ridge VAR(1) is fitted to five World Bank world-aggregate indicators for 2005-2023 (n = 17 transitions). Rolling-origin validation shows no forecasting skill over a mean baseline (overall skill +0.001), the fitted spectral radius is 0.094 (90% bootstrap interval 0.049-0.204), and the headline GDP lag-1 coefficient b =-0.516 (t =-2.33) falls to b =-0.273 (t =-1.11) when three COVID-affected pairs are removed. We therefore report the framework as a validated mathematical model and a negative first empirical result: world-aggregate annual data are too short to identify cross-indicator dynamics. A panel extension is proposed.
