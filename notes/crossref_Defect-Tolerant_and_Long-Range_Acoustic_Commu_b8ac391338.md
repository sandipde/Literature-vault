## Summary
Abstract Plugged and abandoned (P&A) wells require long-term integrity monitoring; however, existing techniques are primarily designed for one-time diagnostics rather than embedded communication through cement. This paper investigates acoustic communication through a representative cemented P&A well structure using a 9.45m annular laboratory model with embedded transmitter and receiver nodes. Frequency-response measurements identified 8kHz as the preferred carrier frequency. A severe extracted defect introduced at 1.37m increased signal attenuation; however, acoustic communication remained achievable over 9.45m as demonstrated by the measured SNR and BER performance under defective conditions. Among the investigated analog modulation methods, frequency modulation (FM) provides the best performance, maintaining a signal-to-noise ratio of 19.7dB at 9.45m in the presence of a defect. Among the digital modulation methods, quadrature phase-shift keying (QPSK) provides the best overall performance, achieving a bit-error rate of 1.1 × 10−3 and the highest estimated bandwidth-limited data-rate upper bound of 8kbps within the 4kHz to 12kHz usable channel. These results demonstrate acoustic communication through a representative cemented P&A structure over 9.45m and establish the feasibility of defect-tolerant embedded acoustic telemetry in a representative P&A well structure. Beyond P&A wells, this method may enable future acoustic communication and monitoring in other cement-based structures, including bunkers, underground tunnels, concrete barriers, and civil infrastructure.
