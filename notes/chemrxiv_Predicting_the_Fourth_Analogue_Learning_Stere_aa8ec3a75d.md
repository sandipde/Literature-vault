## Summary
Combining stereochemical edits offers a direct way to explore cyclic-peptide chemical space. Once a reference peptide and two single-edit analogues have been measured, the modeling question is how molecular structure improves prediction beyond those three observations. We define this conditional permeability task using 1,154 chemically certified squares from four publications and evaluate transfer across 22 complete chemical families. The three measured analogues provide an accurate local anchor: adjacent averaging reduces source–family balanced mean absolute error from 0.319 for additive completion to 0.234 log-permeability units. Structural learning improves this informed prediction. A monomer-alignment difference kernel reaches 0.211 under family holdout, a 10% reduction, and kernelneighbor calibration reaches 0.217 under family holdout and 0.227 under source holdout. Supervision offers a second route to the gain: with the peptide representation fixed, changing raw-difference squared loss to hybrid pinball loss reduces source-held-out error from 0.285 to 0.232. Matched controls connect these improvements to the combination of structural representation, learning target and calibration by measured neighbors. Shared-edit and path-consistency diagnostics distinguish the information used by a correction, while the observed edit–background coverage identifies a concrete next experiment: crossing the same edits across new families under one assay protocol. The study establishes the predictive value of structural learning beyond three measured analogues and provides an independently usable task for testing that contribution. Scientific contribution. This work turns cyclic-peptide edit prediction into a fixed-information task in which local measurements anchor the target and learned structural relationships supply additional accuracy. Chemically certified queries, complete-family holdouts and matched learning comparisons identify how that additional information can be acquired and evaluated.
