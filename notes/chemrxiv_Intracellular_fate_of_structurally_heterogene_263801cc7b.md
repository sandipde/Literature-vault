## Summary
Carbon nitride materials are increasingly investigated for photocatalysis, environmental remediation, and emerging biomedical applications. Although the biological behavior of carbon nitride quantum dots has been studied quite extensively, interactions of larger and structurally heterogeneous carbon nitride structures with cells remain poorly understood. Here, we investigate the intracellular fate of two carbon nitride samples possessing markedly different characteristic dimensions, spanning a range from approximately 100 nm to several micrometers. Confocal microscopy and flow cytometry demonstrated pronounced concentration-and time-dependent cellular accumulation of both samples in HeLa cells. Pharmacological inhibition experiments indicated that uptake is largely mediated by actin-dependent phagocytosis. Super-resolution by optical reassignment imaging and transmission electron microscopy confirmed intracellular localization of carbon nitride structures within membranebound organelles. Live-cell imaging showed progressive accumulation of internalized carbon nitride structures within the lysosomal compartment, consistent with trafficking through the endolysosomal pathway. The larger carbon nitride structures exhibited greater intracellular persistence than their smaller counterparts. Despite efficient uptake, vesicular localization, intracellular processing, and prolonged retention, neither cell proliferation nor cellular stress pathways (interrogated through canonical markers for endoplasmic reticulum stress, oxidative stress, apoptosis, and DNA damage) showed substantial alterations after 12 hours under non-illuminated conditions. These findings demonstrate that structurally heterogeneous carbon nitride architectures remain biologically accessible across a broad size range and provide new insight into their intracellular fate and short-term biosafety.
