## Summary
Predicting the performance characteristics of new materials, including metamaterials created by "assembling" component elements in a specific order, requires the ability to implement mathematical modeling of their properties based on knowledge of the true interatomic interaction potential. A common drawback of existing models is the inability to explicitly account for the nature of changes in the asymmetry of the interatomic interaction potential without using fitting parameters directly related to the anharmonicity of atomic vibrations, which determines the dynamics of the thermal and elastic properties of matter. This paper demonstrates that such a parameter is the coefficient of thermal expansion, directly reflecting the nature of changes in the anharmonicity of the interaction potential, the magnitude of which varies by orders of magnitude from 0 K to the melting temperature. A new approach to solving the problem of determining the anharmonic contribution to the interatomic interaction potential of condensed matter and its temperature dependence is presented. This method is based on established empirical regularities relating kinetic coefficients to the product of the thermal expansion coefficient and temperature for each equilibrium state. The procedure for deriving an interpolation expression describing the temperature dependence of the anharmonic contribution to the interatomic interaction potential is presented. The results of calculating this contribution for five metals from various groups of the periodic table and the dynamics of its increase with temperature are presented. It is shown that below the Debye temperature, the anharmonic contribution changes by tens of orders of magnitude, while above this temperature, the rate of increase slows significantly, which is consistent with the behavior of various physical properties.
