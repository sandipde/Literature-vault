# The Hg + O → HgO Reaction on the Triplet Potential Energy Surface: A CCSD(T)/VTST Investigation

## Summary
The gas-phase reaction of atomic mercury (Hg) with ground-state atomic oxygen (O(3P)) is relevant to mercury oxidation in combustion flue gases and non-thermal plasma environments. However, no experimental rate constants for this reaction are available in the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Chemical Kinetics Database. Here, the reaction is investigated using density functional theory at the B3LYP-D3(BJ)/SDD+6-311++G(3df,3pd) level, combined with CCSD(T) single-point energy calculations. A relaxed potential energy surface (PES) scan was performed along the Hg–O coordinate from 1.80 to 10.00 Å on the spin-free triplet surface. Within this range, the calculated potential energy decreases as the atoms approach, and no entrance-channel barrier above the separated-reactant energy is observed at the sampled points. The equilibrium Hg–O bond length is 2.14 Å, and the dissociation energy D0 is 7.54 kcal mol-1. Variational RRKM calculations using generalized transition states yield a rate constant of 6.47 × 10-10 cm3 molecule-1 s-1 at 300 K, with weak temperature dependence over 300–500 K. The calculated rate constants are compared with Wilcox’s forward association rate expression, accounting for differences in electronic-state descriptions and kinetic treatments. These results provide kinetic data for the Hg + O reaction at low temperatures and a basis for evaluating the atomic-oxygen pathway in mercury oxidation models relevant to non-thermal plasma applications.
