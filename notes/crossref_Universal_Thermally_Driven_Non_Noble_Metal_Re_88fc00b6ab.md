## Summary
ABSTRACT Efficient and durable CO 2 electrolysis in solid oxide electrolysis cells (SOECs) requires cathodes that integrate high activity with robust stability. Herein, a thermally‐driven non‐noble‐metal redistribution strategy is proposed to overcome the long‐standing limitation in dispersing non‐noble metals on perovskite surfaces, enabling perovskite–ceria composite cathodes with dynamically optimized active sites. Thermal treatment of Cu‐dispersed CeO 2 (Cu 0.05 Ce 0.95 O 2 , 5CC) coupled with Sr 2 Fe 1.5 Mo 0.5 O 6− δ (SFM) induces spontaneous Cu migration and redistribution, which can tailor the electronic structure, increase oxygen vacancies and extend triple‐phase boundaries, thereby enhancing CO 2 adsorption, activation and O 2− /e − transport. As a result, a single cell with 5CC–SFM cathode achieves exceptional current densities of 4.14 A cm −2 at 1.5 V under 850°C, respectively. Owing to strong interaction between the redistributed Cu species and perovskite oxide, the single cell maintains stable catalytic performance for 400 h under harsh high‐temperature CO 2 electrolysis conditions. Moreover, this work demonstrates the extended applicability of 5CC–SFM cathode in reversible solid oxide cells (RSOCs) and establishes the generality and methodological significance of thermally‐driven redistribution in achieving highly dispersed non‐noble‐metal species on oxide supports. These findings offer a general framework for the rational design of next‐generation CeO 2 –containing SOEC cathodes and advanced composite materials.
