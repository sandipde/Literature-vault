## Summary
Protein adsorption at fluid interfaces is central to pharmaceutical formulation, food colloids, and bioprocessing technologies, yet its molecular basis remains poorly understood. We investigate lysozyme (LSZ) adsorption at water-air and water-hexadecane interfaces using molecular dynamics simulations with the all-atom CHARMM36m, united-atom GROMOS53A6, and coarse-grained MARTINI 3 force fields. At the waterair interface, only GROMOS53A6 predicts adsorption of native LSZ within the simulated timescales, whereas CHARMM36m and MARTINI 3 show no stable adsorption within 200 ns and 1 µs, respectively. In contrast, all three models predict adsorption at the water-hexadecane interface, consistent with a stronger thermodynamic driving force for adsorption at oil than at vapor interfaces.
