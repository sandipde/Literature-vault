# How Effective is DFTB for Investigating Electrochemical Interfaces? A Study of Metal-Water Systems

## Summary
Density-functional tight binding (DFTB) has recently emerged as a computationally efficient alternative to density functional theory (DFT) for modelling metal-water interfaces. However, electrochemical interfaces are inherently grand-canonical, and it remains unclear whether DFTB can reliably capture potential-dependent electronic response beyond equilibrium properties. Here, we systematically benchmark standard self-consistent-charge DFTB for Au(111) and many-body ChIMES-DFTB for Pt(111) against grand-canonical DFT. Using H2O, OH, and H adsorbates in vacuum and under explicit solvation, we compare two charge-control strategies available in DFTB+: an electronic-constraint counter-electrode approach and a fixed-total-charge scheme. Adsorption energies, Fermi-level response, charge partitioning, and interfacial forces are evaluated across charged interfaces. The electronic-constraint approach reproduces the potential-dependent variation of the Fermi level more faithfully than the fixed-charge scheme, whereas both approaches yield similar adsorption energies and charge partitioning on fixed geometries. We further show that adsorption energetics depend more strongly on the underlying Slater-Koster parameterization than on the addition of many-body ChIMES corrections. Explicit solvation remains the principal limitation, amplifying errors in electronic response and interfacial properties. These findings identify key priorities for developing transferable DFTB models for realistic grand-canonical electrochemical simulations.
