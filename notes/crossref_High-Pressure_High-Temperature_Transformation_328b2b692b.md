## Summary
High-pressure high-temperature (HPHT) transformation of organic and heteroorganic systems has emerged as a versatile platform for the synthesis of diamond materials with tailored structural, optical, isotopic, spin, and biomedical functionalities. This work summarizes the results of our investigations on the synthesis and characterization of diamond materials produced via HPHT-induced transformations of hydrocarbon, fluorinated hydrocarbon, and heteroorganic systems. Particular attention is devoted to the mechanisms of diamond formation, the hydrogen–fluorine synergy responsible for stabilizing nanoscale diamond fractions, and the incorporation of optically active color centers during crystal growth. The formation and photophysical properties of nanodiamonds containing silicon-vacancy (SiV), germanium-vacancy (GeV), and nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers are discussed together with size-dependent optical and quantum phenomena, spatial distributions of color centers, and light–matter interaction effects arising in nanoscale diamond particles. The review further examines isotopically engineered 13C-enriched diamonds and core–shell nanodiamond architectures designed for spin-based applications and hyperpolarization technologies. The capability of HPHT synthesis to independently control particle size, impurity incorporation, isotopic composition, and spin environment provides a versatile platform for engineering advanced diamond materials optimized for quantum photonics, nanoscale sensing, biomedical imaging, and spin-based technologies.
