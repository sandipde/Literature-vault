# Galvanic Coupling Links Heterogeneous and Molecular Palladium Catalysis in Aerobic Alcohol Oxidation

## Summary
Thermochemical catalytic reactions often consist of coupled electrochemical half-reactions, which can even serve to generate the active catalyst itself. Here we show that oxygen reduction on heterogeneous palladium drives corrosion of Pd through galvanic coupling: oxygen reduction consumes the electrons released by oxidation of Pd(0) on the same catalyst, while the soluble Pd(II) forms a homogeneous complex which facilitates benzyl alcohol oxidation. Combining corrosion measurements with quantification of Pd(II) and reaction products, we find that supplying more O 2 raises the catalyst potential and accelerates Pd(II) generation and alcohol oxidation rates. Applying equivalent electrochemical potentials in the absence of O 2 reproduces Pd(II) generation, and alcohol oxidation rates follow a similar dependence on the catalyst potential, which suggests that Pd corrosion is the kinetic link to thermochemical turnover. Diverting the anodic reaction to a sacrificial anode suppresses Pd(II) generation and thus the thermochemical reaction rate. Hence, O 2 has two roles: it sets the potential for forming the molecular catalyst and regenerates Pd(II) after alcohol oxidation. Together, our results show that galvanic coupling provides a framework that links electrochemistry and thermochemical catalysis.
