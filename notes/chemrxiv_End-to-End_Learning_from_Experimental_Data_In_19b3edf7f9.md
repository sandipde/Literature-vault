## Summary
Accurate and computationally efficient thermodynamic models are essential for the design, optimization, and operation of many (bio)chemical processes involving polymer solutions, blends, and membranes. In this work, we combine machine learning (ML) with the PC-SAFT equation of state (EoS) to predict the non-associating pure component parameters for polymers, enabling the rapid evaluation of the pressure–volume–temperature (PVT) behavior of polymer melts and rubbers. Using a dataset of more than 5,400 PVT measurements for 78 chemically diverse polymers, we systematically evaluate the effects of EoS integration in the training process, the molecular representation, neural network architecture, and transfer learning. A baseline approach trained on pre-fitted PC-SAFT parameters is compared with a physics-informed approach that embeds the EoS in the training loop, enabling endto-end training directly on experimental PVT data. Performance is assessed using 6-fold cross-validation. For a feed-forward neural network (FNN) using extendedconnectivity fingerprints (ECFPs), embedding the EoS reduces the average mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) from 8.67% to 5.57%.
