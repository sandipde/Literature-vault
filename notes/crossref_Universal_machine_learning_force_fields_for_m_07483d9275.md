## Summary
Abstract Machine learning force fields (MLFFs) can achieve first-principles accuracy within validated chemical and configurational domains while maintaining computational efficiency comparable to classical force fields for atomistic materials simulations. Currently, MLFFs are evolving from system-specific models toward universal MLFFs. This review summarizes recent progress in universal MLFFs from data construction, model architecture, fine-tuning strategy, physical priors, and complex-system applications. We focus on three reliability bottlenecks: potential energy surface softening due to biased near-equilibrium pretraining data, catastrophic forgetting during downstream fine-tuning, and insufficient treatment of long-range electrostatics, dispersion, and polarization. Representative datasets and benchmarks are discussed together with typical universal MLFFs. We further review forgetting-aware fine-tuning strategies, physics-enhanced frameworks and polarizable long-range models, as well as representative applications in battery electrolytes, solid-state interfaces, ferroelectric phase transitions, phonons, defects and thermal transport. Finally, future directions are discussed toward multifidelity data fusion, task-oriented benchmarks, uncertainty quantification, interpretability and integrated simulation toolchains.
