# Analytical approach to population, polarization and coherence dynamics of chirality induced spin selectivity model

## Summary
We develop an analytical theory of chirality-induced spin selectivity (CISS) for two-electron radical-pair systems, treating spin-state preparation, coherent evolution, and spin-selective recombination within a unified framework. Starting from a Hamiltonian containing isotropic exchange, Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya (DM) interaction, and average and differential local fields, we derive exact two–level dynamics for singlet-triplet populations, spin polarization, and coherence, and compare chirality-induced spin polarization (CISP) with chirality-induced spin coherence (CISC). We then formulate the complete four–level problem in the singlet–Cartesian-triplet basis and obtain analytical expressions for polarization, antisymmetric spin correlation, triplet magnetization, concurrence, and the energy spectrum. The relative phase of the CISS-prepared state determines which coherence quadrature carries the initial spin information and which microscopic interactions can interfere during the subsequent dynamics. For spin-selective recombination, the strong-recombination quantum-Zeno leakage is shown to be governed by the Hamiltonian variance of the reactive bright state, providing a basis-independent measure of bright-dark mixing. The theory further clarifies exchange-DM interference, transverse-channel lifting of two-level dark conditions, and the role of non-coplanar spin geometry in the four-level spectrum.
