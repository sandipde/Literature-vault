## Summary
Machine learning models can help guide reaction development, but their usefulness is often limited by the scarcity of training data in the chemical spaces where predictions are most needed. Active learning can reduce this burden by selecting a minimal number of maximally informative experiments. However, standard acquisition strategies such as Bayesian active learning by disagreement (BALD) prioritize uncertainty without regard to the specific prediction domain of interest. Here, we approach active learning for reaction outcome prediction using expected predictive information gain (EPIG), a target-aware acquisition function that scores candidate experiments according to the predictive information they are expected to provide about a specified target distribution. In a Wittig olefination transfer learning task, EPIG adapts a model trained on established reactions compiled from a foundational Wittig textbook to a distinct literature-sourced reaction domain using approximately half as many acquired reactions as BALD. Across 118 retrospective active learning tasks spanning four reaction datasets, four split families, and targets including yield, enantioselectivity, and stereoselectivity, EPIG achieves the strongest early learning performance on the majority of tasks, remains statistically competitive on nearly all tasks, and achieves the greatest reduction in required data relative to established acquisition strategies and baseline methods. These results demonstrate how target-aware active learning can improve the data efficiency of reaction outcome prediction across diverse chemical settings.
