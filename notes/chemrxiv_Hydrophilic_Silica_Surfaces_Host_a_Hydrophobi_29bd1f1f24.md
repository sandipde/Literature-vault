# Hydrophilic Silica Surfaces Host a Hydrophobic-Like Two-Dimensional Water Network

## Summary
Silica–water interfaces are widely considered prototypically hydrophilic due to surface silanol groups enabling hydrogen bonding with interfacial water. However, this description primarily accounts for vertical surface–water interactions and neglects possible collective in-plane/horizontal organization of interfacial water. Using DFT-MD combined with graph theory, we show that water at crystalline and amorphous SiO2 surfaces systematically forms a highly collective two-dimensional hydrogen-bonded network within the Binding Interfacial Layer (BIL). This extended lateral network is a structural feature typically associated with hydrophobic interfaces, yet it coexists with heterogeneous vertical hydrogenbonding motifs involving surface silanols that anchor the network without disrupting its connectivity. These results demonstrate that silica wettability cannot be reduced to surface–water interactions alone, but emerges from the interplay between vertical anchoring and lateral water–water hydrogen-bond organization, suggesting a revised molecular framework for oxide–water interfaces. This might represent a paradigm shift in the molecular-level description of wettability at hydrophilic oxide interfaces.
