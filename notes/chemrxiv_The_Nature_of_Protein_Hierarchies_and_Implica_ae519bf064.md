## Summary
Proteins are widely described as hierarchies of nested structural units, and hierarchical models are widely used to simulate them, yet what kind of hierarchy proteins actually possess, and what that hierarchy implies for how they should be modelled, has not been established. This work addresses these two questions directly. It builds on three earlier studies from this laboratory, on foldability of lattice proteins, on the relationship between folding and function, and on elastic network models (GNM and ANM) of real proteins, which established hierarchical modularity but left its physical nature and modelling consequences open. Using statistical mechanics (exactly solvable hierarchical foldon models, lattice simulations, residue-level folding ensembles and harmonic elastic networks), an information-theoretic approach (tree decomposition of total correlation, mutual information and exact free-energy cycles) and unsupervised learning (variational autoencoders), and calibrating every index against compact-polymer nulls, matched alternative trees, optimized flat partitions and a frozen protocol on reserved protein families, we find that the hierarchy of proteins is mechanical and organizational rather than thermodynamic.
