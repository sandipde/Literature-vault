# From geometric curvature to effective backgrounds: a dictionary for Dirac fermions in one, two and three spatial dimensions

## Summary
Dirac equation on curved backgrounds can be rewritten as a flat-space equation in which geometry appears as a tensorial Fermi velocity together with a set of background fields. We derive this correspondence from a single Clifford-algebra identity and follow it through one, two, and three spatial dimensions. Once the spin connection is written in terms of Ricci rotation coefficients, only two contractions can occur. The trace produces a term with the index structure of a vector potential in every dimensionality; it enters with an imaginary factor i and is precisely the term that keeps the operator Hermitian when the Fermi velocity depends on position. The totally antisymmetric contraction produces an object that depends on the number of indices available to the Levi-Civita symbol: a trace on a line, a pseudoscalar mass on a surface, and an axial vector potential in a volume. Neither contraction is invariant under local rotations of the frame, and we give the transformation laws. On a surface, the curl of the trace term can always be removed by a frame rotation, while its divergence is proportional to the Gaussian curvature. We apply the correspondence to a Gaussian bump, where the local density of states at the center falls below its flat value and obeys a closed form at low energy; to a wire, where a nonuniform lapse binds states inside the gap and a nonuniform velocity cannot; to stationary frames on a surface, where a circulating drag shifts the Dirac mass locally with either sign; and to twisted and rotating frames in a volume, where the axial term takes the form of a chiral chemical potential produced by the frame alone, or separates Weyl nodes in a rotating sample. We separate the geometric contribution from the lattice pseudogauge field that dominates the response of strained samples
