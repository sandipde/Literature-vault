## Summary
The same local modification can enhance cyclic-peptide permeability in one chemical background 2 and reduce it in another. Molecular difference learning predicts property changes from structure 3 pairs; the question here is what the relationships between an edited residue and its surroundings 4 add to that prediction. We introduce an edit-relational Gaussian process that encodes the residue 5 relationships gained and lost through a graph-certified modification, retaining stereochemistry and 6 native connectivity. In retrospective replay of 286 parent pools and 4,213 measured parent–product 7 pairs, relational inputs improve two-measurement gain over a matched descriptor Gaussian process 8 in all five chemical components. Their component-weighted mean gain is 0.588 log10 permeability 9 units, versus 0.537 for an ExtraTrees descriptor ranker at the same budget and 0.491 for four random 10 measurements. Controls with a shared first mean-ranked choice locate the principal measured benefit in 11 the representation and initial ranking. Measured stereochemical reversals across methylation backgrounds 12 motivate prospective tests of whether solvent-dependent amide exposure predicts where edit benefits 13 transfer. Changed residue relationships thus supply predictive distinctions that help a model recognize 14 when the chemical background changes the value of an edit. 15 Scientific contribution. We extend molecular difference learning with the residue relationships 16 changed by a certified local edit. Matched Gaussian-process comparisons show that these inputs add 17 predictive value beyond parent physicochemical descriptors and their changes. Measured interaction 18 panels identify the background dependence that this representation seeks to capture, while finite-pool 19 analysis separates candidate resolution from measurement allocation. 20
