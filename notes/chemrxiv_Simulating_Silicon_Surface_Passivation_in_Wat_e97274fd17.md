## Summary
The passivation of silicon surfaces is important in several industrial settings, such as for semiconductor device fabrication and photovoltaic applications. In this study, we compare molecular dynamics (MD) simulations based on the ReaxFF reactive force field and fine-tuned Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials (MLIPs) to extend our understanding of mechanism of Si(100) surface passivation in presence of water at various temperatures (300K, 350K, 383K). Fine-tuning was performed via DFT-based ab initio MD (AIMD) simulations of the full solid-liquid interface. Simulation results indicate that the surface termination occurs predominantly through dissociative adsorption leading to the formation of silanol-terminated silica networks on the surface. At higher temperatures the atoms possess sufficient kinetic energy to reorganize into thermodynamically more stable structures as compared to room temperature where the hydroxylation is more disordered. The fine-tuned MLIP model confirms the key structural features obtained from ReaxFF, validating the latter as an approach for capturing essential passivation behavior. These findings provide atomistic insights into water-driven silicon passivation and may guide room-temperature passivation strategies. Our strategy also highlights a computationally efficient approach to the passivation of reactive surfaces, as a preliminary and necessary step in numerous theoretical studies. This protocol can be also utilized for wider varieties of Solid–Electrolyte Interphase (SEI) system, to prepare a realistic, passivated surfaces for advance theoretical studies.
