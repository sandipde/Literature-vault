## Summary
International healthcare students experience additional stressors during mandated work-integrated learning (WIL), compared to domestic students. A key identified obstacle for international healthcare students can be the development of work-based relationships with healthcare staff and patients, due to language and culture differences (Hari et al., 2021; Oikarainen et al., 2018; Yu et al., 2023). Strategies to address these difficulties have included communication skills training (Brewer et al., 2024; Chan et al., 2016; Merry et al., 2021), accent-related communication (Chan et al., 2016) and cultural competence (Merry et al., 2021). While communication is critical, WIL incorporates many different physical, social and emotional learning components. A teaching method that encompasses contemporary learning theories, whilst capturing clinical and interpersonal skill development, is simulation-based learning (SBL) (Judd et al., 2023; Ludvig et al., 2024). SBL is widely used in healthcare training, however there are few comprehensive reviews of SBL approaches specifically for international healthcare students. This Pecha Kutcha presentation will present the process of designing a SBL intervention for nursing students, through a design-based research (DBR) framework.
