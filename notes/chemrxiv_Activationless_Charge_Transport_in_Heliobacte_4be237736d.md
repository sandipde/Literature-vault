# Activationless Charge Transport in Heliobacterial Reaction Centers: Polarizable Cofactors Coupled to Strong Intraprotein Electric Fields

- **Published:** 2026-11-16

## Summary
We study rate-limiting steps of electron transfer following photoinduced charge separation to the primary acceptor A0 in a heliobacterial reaction center (HbRC). Two steps involved are electron transfer to the iron-sulfur cluster (ISC) FX of HbRC, followed by electron transfer to the FA ISC of a ferredoxin protein in solution. Ferredoxin forms a stable binding configuration with the reaction center as established by Brownian dynamics simulation. Molecular dynamics simulations are used to calculate the reaction times of ∼ 0.1µs (A0 → FX) and ∼ 3− 5µs (FX → FA). These two steps are the slowest in the overall electron transport in HbRC. Electron transfer to ferredoxin is irreversible since the calculated time of recombination to the closest A0 cofactor is ∼ 70 ms. All reactions show near-zero activation barriers when cofactors’ polarizabilities are included in the calculations. The overall robust operation of solar energy conversion in HbRCs is allowed by combining cofactors’ polarizabilities with strong local intraprotein electric fields.
