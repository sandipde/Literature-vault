## Summary
This study presents a theoretical investigation of phase transitions in carbon systems based on polymerized fullerite using a machine-learned interatomic potential of the Moment Tensor Potentials (MTP). The relevance of this work stems from the lack of reliable atomistic models of the new carbon material (NCM), which necessitates the use of physically grounded model systems capable of reproducing key elements of its structure. A polymerized C60/C70-based fullerite was selected as such a system, combining the molecular organization of the initial structures with the possibility of transitioning to covalently bonded configurations with partial sp3 hybridization. The developed potential was parameterized based on density functional theory data, including results from ab initio molecular dynamics, and demonstrates good accuracy in reproducing energies and forces for a wide range of carbon configurations. This allowed us to perform simulations in the wide range of pressures and temperatures with final obtaining a phase diagram of the system. It is shown that the formation of stable polymerized phases is associated with a region of elevated pressures, where the transition from the molecular state to a bonded network becomes energetically favorable. Analysis of atomistic dynamics revealed that the structural transformation is heterogeneous in nature and occurs through a sequential redistribution of electron density, accompanied by a transition from localized π-bonds to a more isotropic σ-bonded configuration. This leads to the formation of spatially cross-linked regions with increased stiffness. The calculated elastic properties show that such structures exhibit a Young’s modulus of ~300 GPa. The obtained results allow us to refine the physical picture of fullerene polymerization and provide a basis for targeted modeling of more complex carbon phases with a diamond-like framework.
