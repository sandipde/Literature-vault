# Local Chemistry of Na-Site Substitution in P2−NaxMn0.66Ni0.33O2 Cathodes for Na-ion Batteries. A First-Principles Study of Structural Fluctuations, Ion Mobility, and Charge Redistribution

## Summary
Layered oxides emerge as promising materials as cathode in sodium ion batteries due to the possibility to avoid critical metals and the direct industrial transferability in lithium-ion battery factories. A key point is that the layered structure can host a wide range of cations, enabling the substitution as a strategy to improve key factors such as structural stability, electronic conductivity, ion mobility, etc. For that, it important to determine how the different substituents can affect these factors, to then mitigate side reactions and improve the desired mechanisms. In the present work, the effect of the interlayer substituent ions on the open circuit voltage, cation mobility, local structural fluctuations, and electronic structure has been investigated for P 2 − Na x Mn 0.66 Ni 0.33 O 2 ( with x = 0.66 and 0.083) cathodes through Density Functional Theory. Calculated open-circuit voltages show that the dopants do not significantly alter the overall desodiation potential, while the local voltage response depends on the substituent identity and the sodium content. Ab initio molecular dynamics simulations reveal that all dopants maintain the layered topology while inducing distinct local dynamical responses, with Mg and Zn producing the most rigid local environments and K the highest lattice flexibility. CI-NEB calculations and Arrhenius-based estimation of diffusion rates show that K facilitates the Na-ion hopping, and Ca maintains Na-ion migration barriers comparable to those of the pristine material, whereas Mg and Zn significantly increase the activation energy for Na inter site-hopping. Electronic structure analysis reveals a markedly different behavior between isovalent and aliovalent substitutions: at high Na content, divalent dopants locally promote Mn 3+ -like electronic configurations, while at low Na content the charge is primarily accommodated by the oxygen sublattice; in both regimes, divalent dopants enhance oxygen participation in charge compensation. These results provide atomistic insight into how Na-site chemistry governs the balance between ionic transport, electronic structure, and structural stability, offering practical guidelines for the local chemistry of high-performance layered sodium-ion cathodes.
