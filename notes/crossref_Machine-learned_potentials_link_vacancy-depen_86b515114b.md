## Summary
Abstract Copper contamination threatens silicon devices because mobile Cu can form electrically active defects and redistribute during thermal processing. Here we combine DFT-informed implantation simulations, an actively learned deep potential (DP) and large-scale molecular dynamics to connect elementary Cu migration with vacancy-dependent transport and annealed depth profiles. The DP reproduces DFT energies and forces with root-mean-square errors of 3.16–3.71 meV/atom and 35.10–88.30 meV/Å, respectively. Nudged-elastic-band calculations yield an interstitial migration barrier of 0.192 eV, close to the DFT value of 0.185 eV, and capture the energetic preference for Cu occupation of a Si vacancy. Across systems containing eight Cu atoms and 0, 2, 4 or 8 Si vacancies, atom-resolved trajectories reveal competition between mobile interstitial Cu and vacancy-associated Cu. Increasing vacancy number raises the prevalence of locally confined Cu and changes the ensemble-averaged transport behaviour. The vacancy-free system has an apparent activation energy of 0.202 eV, comparable in magnitude to reported experimental values of 0.15–0.18 eV. The combined DFT-MD + DP framework also reproduces the principal features of SIMS profiles before and after annealing. Relative to the MC/BCA + KMC workflow assessed here, it reduces mean-normalized NRMSE by 47–61% across the full profiles, with improvements in both the near-surface region and low-concentration tail. By linking Cu–vacancy interactions to measurable redistribution, this framework offers a route to jointly evaluating implantation-induced defects and annealing conditions when seeking to limit mobile Cu and residual Cu–defect complexes in silicon.
