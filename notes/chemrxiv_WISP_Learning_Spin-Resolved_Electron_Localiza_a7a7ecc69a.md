## Summary
The electron localization function (ELF) has been used to identify bonds, lone pairs, and interstitial electrides, in materials ranging from thermoelectrics, batteries, and superhydrides. We train WISP to predict the spin-resolved electron localization function (ELF) from the atomic geometry and signed site moments of a periodic crystal. One network predicts both spin channels at any point in the crystal, using opposite signs of the moments. To improve accuracy in layered and porous materials, a far-field branch includes information from atoms beyond the local cutoff. A separate network predicts the moments when only geometry is available. The per-channel mean absolute error is 0.0106 on the zero to one ELF scale. Errors in the individual channels and their difference are smaller than for a two-head control with the same inputs. The predicted fields reproduce the spin contrast of MnPd and the interstitial maximum in the electride Sc₅Cl₈ without occupied orbitals as inputs, suggesting potential use in screening electrides and magnetic materials.
