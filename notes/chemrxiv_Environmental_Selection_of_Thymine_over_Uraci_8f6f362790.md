# Environmental Selection of Thymine over Uracil in Prebiotic Chemical Evolution: Insights from a Kinetic Monte Carlo Model

## Summary
The replacement of uracil (U) by thymine (T) is a defining chemical feature of the transition from RNA-to DNA-based heredity. While thymine’s superior stability is well known, the role of prebiotic environmental conditions in driving this selection remains quantitatively underexplored. Here we present a stochastic kinetic Monte Carlo model (UPDSF v4.4) that simulates competition between uracil and thymine across four archetypal prebiotic environments using strictly literature-derived parameters. Molecular fitness is quantified by a Stability-Kinetics Ratio (Sr = τ_stable / κ_poly). Simulations show strong, environment-dependent selection: thymine is preferentially preserved in high-temperature hydrothermal, UV-exposed, and wet-dry cycling regimes due to its enhanced resistance to hydrolysis, deamination, and photodimerization. In contrast, uracil retains a kinetic advantage in lipid-assisted cryogenic eutectic phases where concentration effects and lower polymerization activation energy favor its incorporation. These results indicate that heterogeneous prebiotic environments could have 2 imposed deterministic physicochemical filters favoring thymine in DNA precursors. The model provides a quantitative platform for integrating kinetic data into origins-of-life scenarios and highlights the importance of spatial and temporal environmental heterogeneity.
