## Summary
We estimate how nuclear delocalization affects magnesium and calcium release from occupied wells in the naltriben-open pore of transient receptor potential melastatin 7 (TRPM7). We compare the equilibrium distributions of sodium, magnesium, and calcium in the same open structure and fixed continuum field. Thermal quantum states extend over nearby positions, so the ion can sample more than one local environment. For the divalent ions, the model combines a Gaussian test charge in the nonlinear Poisson--Boltzmann potential, spatial dielectric self-energy, and the free-energy cost of restricting a correlated first hydration shell. The hydration shell comprises the water molecules that directly coordinate the ion. In a constriction, the wall can block access to some of these water sites. The classical and quantum calculations use the same insertion energy, pore geometry, and axial diffusivity. The leading Wigner--Kirkwood correction for a smooth ion energy $U_i$ away from the wall is $\ell_i^2\left[\nabla^2 U_i-\frac{\beta}{2}|\nabla U_i|^2\right]$, up to an additive constant, with $\ell_i^2=\frac{\beta\hbar^2}{12m_i}$ for ion mass $m_i$ and inverse thermal energy $\beta$. The length $\ell_i$ is about $0.02\,\text{\AA}$ at $310\,\text{K}$. At the point-ion magnesium basin, the refined centerline self-energy reaches $430\,\text{meV}$. Steric constraints also restrict water coordination at the $1.43\,\text{\AA}$ constriction. At $310\,\text{K}$ in the primary extended dielectric box, the deepest wells for magnesium and calcium lie at $22.75$ and $11.00\,\text{\AA}$, respectively. The quantum changes in mean passage time toward the intracellular and extracellular interfaces are $0.68\%$ and $-0.66\%$ for magnesium and $-0.10\%$ and $16.12\%$ for calcium. Calcium starts on the intracellular side of the constriction and must cross it to reach the extracellular interface. The preferred magnesium site is already on the extracellular side. A change in the dielectric boundary for magnesium or the ligand penalty for calcium can move the deepest well across the constriction and reverse the extracellular response. For comparison, the circular point-ion estimate for magnesium changes from $-37.4\%$ and $-34.5\%$ to $-8.9\%$ and $-8.6\%$ with a $0.5\,\text{\AA}$ wall inset. In the circular reference, the largest local correction occurs at the intracellular widening and depends on near-wall access and axial sampling. Its influence on escape from the occupied well is small. In a separate screened two-ion benchmark, classical repulsion changes escape barriers by tens to hundreds of meV. Isotope substitution tests the mass dependence at fixed charge and chemistry. Together, the results show how pore geometry, dielectric response, and water coordination determine which local quantum corrections affect directional release.
