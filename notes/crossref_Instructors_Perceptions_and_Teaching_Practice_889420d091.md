# Instructors' Perceptions and Teaching Practices Regarding Student Engagement in Online Active Learning Environments: The Case of Minerva Fully Active Learning Courses

## Summary
This study examined instructors' perceptions and teaching practices regarding student engagement in online active learning, focusing on the strategies they use and the difficulties and growth they experience. Fully Active Learning courses jointly operated by University A and the Minerva Project were selected as the case. Data were collected through semi-structured individual interviews with six instructors who had taught these courses for at least one semester. The analysis yielded three themes, seven categories, and 15 subcategories. First, instructors experienced a shift in how they understood active learning and online and offline modes of instruction. This led to changes in their instructional practices, including extending trust and autonomy to students, transferring and expanding their experiences to other courses, and managing class time more effectively. Second, instructors flexibly drew on a wide range of strategies to promote students' cognitive, emotional, and behavioral engagement. These strategies included question-based facilitation, correcting misconceptions, adjusting task difficulty, creating a safe learning environment, providing emotional support, cold-calling, and monitoring breakout group activities. Third, collaboration with colleagues allowed instructors to complement each other's expertise and maintain the quality of instruction, though it also required more of their time. This study shows that instructional strategies for promoting student engagement in online active learning are not one-dimensional but multidimensional and multifaceted in practice. It also points to the need for support systems to strengthen instructor competencies and foster collaborative professional development.
