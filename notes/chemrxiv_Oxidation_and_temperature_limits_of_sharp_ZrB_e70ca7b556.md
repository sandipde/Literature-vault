# Oxidation and temperature limits of sharp ZrB2–SiC nose tips in hypersonic flight: a coupled screening model

## Summary
Sharp-edge flight envelopes consider temperature alone, yet sharp ZrB2–SiC nose tips can also fail by losing their protective borosilicate glass or by SiC active oxidation. We couple stagnation-point heating, conduction and a layered oxidation model to map the Mach numbers and altitudes at which a tip lasts 30 minutes. Four mechanistic findings come from comparing runs of the same model, so they depend less on its absolute accuracy than the maps do. First, conduction into the flank is the largest single physical effect, placing the 30-minute boundary of a two-dimensional nose-tip model 0.5–2.75 Mach above that of one-dimensional stagnation-line maps, conditional on the flank heating. Second, the glass evaporation coefficient decides whether glass loss or the temperature limit ends the life but moves the boundary by at most 0.5 Mach. Third, with a passive-to-active line fitted to pooled data, SiC active oxidation is never the first failure at flight oxygen pressures of 2–220 kPa, though low-pressure ground tests can show it. Fourth, the heating, glass emissivity and silica balance control the time to failure in two base cases. The model fails every furnace, arc-jet and plasmatron validation check. Its fully catalytic surface is hotter than every measured one, which is on the safe side, but its glass lasts longer than in the plasmatron. Its absolute 30-minute boundaries are thus screening estimates not shown to be conservative. Because the failure mode depends on oxygen pressure, ground tests need to reproduce flight pressure and gas composition as well as heat flux.
