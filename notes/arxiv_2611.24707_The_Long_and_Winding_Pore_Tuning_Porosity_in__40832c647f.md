# The Long and Winding Pore: Tuning Porosity in Templated Nanoporous Carbons

## Summary
Disordered materials pose a recurring challenge for molecular simulation: realistic structural models are often generated through thermal protocols in which several structural descriptors emerge simultaneously and cannot easily be varied independently. Nanoporous carbons constitute a particularly relevant example: pore size, wall order, curvature, and network tortuosity generally emerge as outcomes of the thermal preparation protocol, rather than being independently prescribed structural parameters. Moreover, for practical applications, models of porous materials should ideally exhibit a percolating pore network, capable of supporting end-to-end transport of adsorbed species through the material. To this end, here we introduce a hierarchy of templating strategies, built around a common melt-quench-anneal protocol driven by a machine-learned interatomic potential for carbon, that progressively separates these variables. A rigid cylindrical template interacting with the environment through a tunable Lennard-Jones potential provides independent control over pore size and local wall organization, while leaving the structural disorder of the surrounding carbon matrix essentially unchanged. Generalizing the template to a spring-connected, tortuous backbone further allows pore geometry and curvature to be prescribed without qualitatively altering the local templating mechanism established for the straight pore. Finally, making the template mobile lets the growing carbon network reshape it in turn, which sharpens pore boundaries and allows sub-nanometer pore sizes to be targeted precisely, as needed for applications such as supercapacitor electrodes. Ultimately, this approach enables systematic studies of structure-property relationships in porous materials for applications such as energy storage, desalination and gas capture.
