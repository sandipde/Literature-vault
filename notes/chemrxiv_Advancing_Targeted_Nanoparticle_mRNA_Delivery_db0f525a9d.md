## Summary
Effective delivery remains a major barrier to translating nucleic acid therapeutics into the clinic. A successful delivery system must cross biological barriers, target the tissue of interest, deliver the potent payload to the cell, and be manufactured with ease at scale. The three clinically relevant carrier classes involve trade-offs with one another: viral vectors are limited by payload capacity and limited redosing; lipid nanoparticles are a clinical standard for mRNA delivery, but default to liver tropism; polymeric nanoparticles have lower immunogenicity and offer shelf-stable manufacturing, but fewer candidates have reached clinical trials. The design of effective nanomedicines requires multi-faceted optimization of nanoparticle properties, targeted delivery, and cargo efficacy. Machine learning offers an opportunity to accelerate nanoparticle development by effectively navigating the design space and providing a deeper insight into structure-activity relationships. Here, we present a high-level overview of the fast evolving nanoparticle delivery field and outline best practices for advancing the development of targeted nanoparticle mRNA delivery using machine learning. The key design principles are introduced: identification of target cell surface receptors, de novo design of epitope-targeted antibodies, design of mRNA payloads, and prediction of nanoparticle properties and their interactions at different biological scales. Together, advancing targeted nanoparticle mRNA delivery will require a coordinated integration of key design principles, the generation of high-quality datasets, and the effective adoption of machine learning.
