# Contact organization separates biological from crystal protein interfaces matched on area and disjoint in sequence

## Summary
Interface area can dominate discrimination between physiological protein interfaces and crystal contacts. We tested whether local contact organization remains informative after area matching and sequence-cluster separation. In 2,000 balanced BioMany/XtalMany interfaces, sequence cluster aware cross validation gave ROC AUC 0.904 for area and 0.937 after adding five normalized contact descriptors (difference 0.033; bootstrap 95% interval 0.015–0.058). We then fixed 40 biological and 40 crystal homomeric interfaces from the Duarte–Capitani collection, matched pairwise on area within 800–2000 Å2 and sharing no 30%-identity cluster with development. Area reached AUC 0.523; the frozen contact score reached 0.849 (dependency-component bootstrap 95% interval 0.751–0.927) and average precision 0.812. Biological interfaces carried 2.5 times the hydrophobic carbon-pair density of crystal contacts, 1.91 versus 0.76 pairs per 100 Å2. Post hoc analysis identified hydrophobic density as the main contributor; this descriptor alone reached AUC 0.829. On the same panel, unchanged PRODIGY-cryst reached AUC 0.8475 and the lineage-linked current EPPIC score reached 0.876. Contact organization therefore discriminated the curated classes after area matching and sequence-cluster separation. The inference applies to EPPIC-derived labels; protein chain size remained unmatched and associated with the score.
